Beginner Training

BUILD CONFIDENCE. START SIMPLE.

Learn the fundamentals with a simple full-body routine built around control, consistency and gradual progress.

01

Overview

This plan is designed for students who are new to structured training or returning after a long break. The goal is not to make every session exhausting, but to become comfortable with basic movements, learn how to use the equipment and build a routine that can be repeated consistently.

Frequency 2–3 sessions / week
Duration 40–55 minutes
Level Beginner

Start with two sessions per week, leaving at least one rest day between sessions. Once the routine feels comfortable, gradually move to three sessions per week.

02

Training approach

Use a resistance that feels manageable from the first repetition to the last. Each movement should remain controlled, with enough energy left to complete another two or three repetitions if needed.

General guide 2 sets · 8–12 reps · 60–90 sec rest

Focus on completing the movement well before adding more weight. When you can comfortably complete the upper end of the repetition range with consistent technique, increase the resistance slightly.

03

Session structure

Warm-up 5–8 min

Begin with easy movement to raise your body temperature and prepare your joints.

  • Brisk walking or light marching
  • Arm circles
  • Hip hinges
  • Bodyweight squats
  • Gentle shoulder and hip mobility
01

Goblet Squat

2 sets · 8–10 reps

Hold one dumbbell or kettlebell close to your chest. Keep your feet comfortable, lower with control and stand tall again.

02

Dumbbell Romanian Deadlift

2 sets · 8–10 reps

Hold the dumbbells in front of your thighs and push your hips backward while keeping your spine comfortably neutral. Return to standing by driving the hips forward.

03

Incline Push-Up or Dumbbell Bench Press

2 sets · 8–10 reps

Choose whichever variation allows you to move through the exercise comfortably and with control.

04

One-Arm Dumbbell Row

2 sets · 8–10 reps each side

Support yourself on the bench if needed and pull the dumbbell toward your side without twisting your torso.

05

Glute Bridge

2 sets · 10–12 reps

Lie on a mat with your knees bent, press through your feet and raise your hips while keeping the movement controlled.

06

Dead Bug

2 sets · 6–8 reps each side

Move the opposite arm and leg away from your body while keeping your lower back stable against the mat.

Cool-down 5 min

Finish with easy walking, relaxed breathing and gentle stretches for the muscles you trained.

04

Keep in mind

Your first few weeks are about learning, not testing your limits. Keep each movement comfortable and controlled. Stop a set if your technique begins to break down, and avoid training through sharp or unusual pain.

Week 1–2

Learn the exercises and establish the routine.

Week 3–4

Gradually add repetitions within the recommended range.

Week 5+

Add a small amount of resistance when the upper repetition range feels comfortable.

You do not need to increase something every session. Progress can also mean moving with better control, feeling more confident with the equipment or completing your routine more consistently.

05

Equipment used

This routine uses a small selection of versatile equipment available at Alphonsians' Gym.

  • Dumbbells
  • Kettlebells
  • Adjustable Weight Bench
  • Yoga Mat

Strength Training

BUILD STRENGTH. MOVE WITH CONTROL.

Develop full-body strength through controlled resistance, consistent technique and gradual progression.

01

Overview

This plan is designed for students who are comfortable with basic movement patterns and want to begin developing greater strength. The focus is on a small group of full-body exercises that can be progressed gradually over time.

Frequency 2–3 sessions / week
Duration 50–65 minutes
Level Beginner–Intermediate

Leave at least one rest day between strength sessions when possible. Recovery is part of the training process and gives your muscles time to adapt before the next session.

02

Training approach

Choose resistance that makes the final few repetitions challenging while still allowing you to maintain stable, controlled technique. Avoid sacrificing movement quality simply to lift more weight.

General guide 3 sets · 6–10 reps · 90–120 sec rest

Complete each repetition deliberately and use the full comfortable range of motion. When all sets can be completed at the upper end of the repetition range with consistent technique, increase the resistance slightly.

03

Session structure

Warm-up 6–8 min

Prepare the major joints and movement patterns before adding resistance.

  • Brisk walking or light movement
  • Bodyweight squats
  • Hip hinges
  • Arm circles and shoulder mobility
  • Light practice sets of the first exercise
01

Goblet Squat

3 sets · 6–10 reps

Hold a dumbbell or kettlebell close to your chest. Lower with control, keep your feet firmly planted and drive through the floor as you return to standing.

02

Barbell Romanian Deadlift

3 sets · 6–8 reps

Hold the bar close to your body and push your hips backward while maintaining a controlled torso position. Stop when you reach a comfortable range, then return to standing.

03

Dumbbell Bench Press

3 sets · 6–10 reps

Lie securely on the bench with your feet planted. Lower the dumbbells with control and press them upward without rushing the movement.

04

One-Arm Dumbbell Row

3 sets · 8–10 reps each side

Support yourself on the bench and pull the dumbbell toward your side. Keep your torso stable and avoid using momentum to move the weight.

05

Dumbbell Split Squat

2–3 sets · 8 reps each side

Stand in a staggered position with a dumbbell in each hand. Lower yourself vertically with control and use the front leg to return to the starting position.

06

Plank

2–3 sets · 20–40 sec

Maintain a stable position from shoulders to hips while breathing normally. End the set when you can no longer hold the position comfortably.

Cool-down 5 min

Finish with easy movement, relaxed breathing and gentle mobility for the areas trained during the session.

04

Progress gradually

Strength develops through repeated, manageable progress rather than large jumps in resistance. Keep the same exercises long enough to become confident with them before making significant changes.

Step 01

Begin with a resistance you can control comfortably throughout every set.

Step 02

Gradually increase repetitions until you reach the upper end of the recommended range.

Step 03

Add a small amount of resistance and return to the lower end of the repetition range.

Not every session needs to be harder than the last. Better technique, greater control and completing the same workload more comfortably are also signs of progress.

05

Equipment used

This routine uses free weights and basic equipment available at Alphonsians' Gym to train the major movement patterns of the body.

  • Dumbbells
  • Barbell & Weight Plates
  • Adjustable Weight Bench
  • Kettlebells
  • Yoga Mat

Weight Control

MOVE OFTEN. STAY CONSISTENT.

Combine strength, steady movement and conditioning in a routine designed to support regular, sustainable activity.

01

Overview

This plan combines resistance exercises with continuous movement and short conditioning intervals. The aim is to build a routine that keeps you active while also supporting strength, stamina and overall fitness.

Frequency 3 sessions / week
Duration 45–60 minutes
Level Beginner–Intermediate

Leave enough time between sessions to recover comfortably. On other days, light activity such as walking, stretching or easy mobility work can help you stay active without turning every day into a demanding workout.

02

Training approach

The goal is to keep the session moving without rushing your technique. Use moderate resistance and move between exercises at a pace you can maintain consistently.

General guide 2–3 sets · 10–12 reps · 45–75 sec rest

Choose resistance that allows you to complete each set with control. The conditioning sections should raise your breathing rate, but you should still be able to recover before beginning the next round.

03

Session structure

Warm-up 6–8 min

Start with easy movement and gradually increase the pace before beginning the main workout.

  • Brisk walking or light marching
  • Bodyweight squats
  • Alternating reverse lunges
  • Arm circles
  • Hip and shoulder mobility
01

Goblet Squat

3 sets · 10–12 reps

Hold a dumbbell or kettlebell close to your chest. Lower with control, keep your feet firmly planted and return smoothly to standing.

02

Dumbbell Romanian Deadlift

3 sets · 10–12 reps

Keep the dumbbells close to your legs as you push your hips backward. Maintain control through the movement and return to standing without rushing.

03

Incline Push-Up

2–3 sets · 8–12 reps

Place your hands securely on the bench and keep your body aligned as you lower yourself toward the surface. Press back up with controlled movement.

04

One-Arm Dumbbell Row

2–3 sets · 10 reps each side

Use the bench for support and pull the dumbbell toward your side while keeping your torso stable. Lower it slowly before repeating.

05

Kettlebell Deadlift

2–3 sets · 10–12 reps

Stand with the kettlebell between your feet. Hinge at the hips, keep the weight close to your body and return to standing with a controlled hip drive.

06

Standing March

2 rounds · 45–60 sec

March continuously at a steady pace, lifting the knees comfortably and allowing the arms to move naturally.

Conditioning 8–12 min

Alternate periods of moderate movement with short recovery periods. Keep the pace challenging but repeatable.

  • 40 sec brisk movement
  • 20 sec easy recovery
  • Repeat for 8–12 rounds
Cool-down 5 min

Reduce the pace gradually with easy walking, relaxed breathing and gentle stretches.

04

Focus on consistency

A routine that you can repeat regularly is more useful than making every session as difficult as possible. Build the habit first, then gradually increase the amount of work you can perform comfortably.

Step 01

Complete the routine at a steady pace and learn how each exercise feels.

Step 02

Gradually increase repetitions or reduce rest slightly when the session begins to feel easier.

Step 03

Add a small amount of resistance or extend the conditioning block while keeping the session manageable.

Progress does not have to come from doing more every time. Completing sessions more regularly, recovering better and maintaining a steady pace are also meaningful signs of improvement.

05

Equipment used

This routine combines simple resistance equipment with bodyweight movement to keep the session flexible and easy to repeat.

  • Dumbbells
  • Kettlebells
  • Adjustable Weight Bench
  • Yoga Mat

Endurance Training

BUILD STAMINA. KEEP MOVING.

Improve stamina through steady movement, controlled intervals and gradual increases in how long you can stay active.

01

Overview

This plan is designed to improve your ability to keep moving for longer periods without becoming overly fatigued. It combines steady activity with moderate intervals so that stamina and recovery can develop gradually over time.

Frequency 2–3 sessions / week
Duration 35–55 minutes
Level Beginner–Intermediate

Begin with an amount of activity that feels manageable and leave enough recovery time between sessions. As your stamina improves, gradually extend how long you move before increasing the intensity.

02

Training approach

Endurance training should feel challenging enough to raise your breathing rate while still allowing you to remain in control. Most of the session should be performed at a pace that you can sustain rather than at maximum effort.

General guide Moderate pace · Controlled intervals · Gradual recovery

During steady sections, aim for a pace where speaking a short sentence is still possible. During intervals, increase the effort slightly before returning to an easier pace for recovery.

03

Session structure

Warm-up 6–8 min

Begin slowly and gradually increase your movement before starting the main endurance work.

  • Easy walking or marching
  • Arm swings and circles
  • Gentle knee lifts
  • Bodyweight squats
  • Hip and ankle mobility
01

Steady Movement

10–15 min

Maintain continuous movement at an easy-to-moderate pace. Use brisk walking, marching or another comfortable movement that allows you to stay active without stopping.

02

Jump Rope or Simulated Skipping

6 rounds · 30 sec work / 30 sec easy

Use a jump rope if comfortable, or perform the same rhythm without the rope. Keep the pace controlled and use the recovery period to bring your breathing back down.

03

Bodyweight Squat

2 rounds · 12–15 reps

Keep a steady rhythm rather than rushing the repetitions. Lower with control and return to standing while maintaining relaxed breathing.

04

Resistance Band Row

2 rounds · 12–15 reps

Pull the band toward your body with a controlled rhythm and return slowly. Choose resistance that allows you to keep moving without straining.

05

Alternating Reverse Lunge

2 rounds · 8–10 reps each side

Step backward into a comfortable lunge and return to standing. Alternate sides while keeping the pace steady and controlled.

06

Standing March

2 rounds · 60 sec

March continuously at a steady rhythm while maintaining an upright posture. Use this as active recovery between harder movements if needed.

Endurance intervals 8–12 min

Finish the main portion of the session by alternating moderate effort with easier active recovery.

  • 45 sec moderate movement
  • 30 sec easy movement
  • Repeat for 6–8 rounds
Cool-down 5–8 min

Gradually reduce the pace with easy walking or marching. Finish with relaxed breathing and gentle mobility once your breathing begins to settle.

04

Build progressively

Endurance improves when the body is given slightly more work over time without increasing everything at once. Begin by becoming comfortable with the current session before extending the duration or increasing the pace.

Step 01

Establish a steady pace that you can maintain throughout the planned session.

Step 02

Add a few minutes to the steady movement or complete an additional interval when the current session feels manageable.

Step 03

Gradually increase the pace or shorten the recovery periods while keeping your movement controlled.

Increase only one element at a time. Longer sessions, faster intervals and shorter recovery periods are all forms of progression, but they do not need to be introduced together.

05

Equipment used

Most of this routine can be completed using bodyweight movements with a small amount of equipment for variety and resistance.

  • Jump Rope
  • Resistance Bands
  • Yoga Mat
